# Review of Rao--Blackwellized filtering and backward smoothing

## 1. Smoothing and backward-sampling literature

### 1.1 Filtering, marginal smoothing, and joint smoothing

For a state-space model with latent states $X_{0:T}$ and observations
$y_{1:T}$, filtering targets

$$
p(X_t\mid y_{1:t}),
$$

whereas fixed-interval smoothing targets past states after conditioning on all
the data,

$$
p(X_t\mid y_{1:T}).
$$

The complete joint smoothing distribution is

$$
p(X_{0:T}\mid y_{1:T}).
$$

Knowing every one-time marginal $p(X_t\mid y_{1:T})$ does not determine this
joint distribution. In particular, independently drawing from the one-time
marginals loses the temporal dependence and does not reproduce lag-one moments
such as

$$
E[X_tX_{t+1}^{\mathsf T}\mid y_{1:T}].
$$

These moments matter for transition-parameter estimation. For the OU model,
the M-step depends on the residual

$$
r_t=X_{t+1}-\mu_0-\phi_t(X_t-\mu_0)
$$

and hence on $E[r_tr_t^{\mathsf T}\mid y_{1:T}]$, which contains both
one-time and lag-one smoothing moments.

The Markov property gives the forward-filtering/backward-simulation (FFBS)
factorization

$$
p(X_{0:T}\mid y_{1:T})
=p(X_T\mid y_{1:T})
\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}p(X_t\mid X_{t+1},y_{1:t}).
$$

Thus a joint path can be generated by drawing $X_T$ first and then drawing
$X_t$ conditionally on the already-drawn $X_{t+1}$. This is the reason for
sampling backward: the future endpoint couples consecutive states and preserves
the path distribution. Backward sampling is not the only possible smoother;
two-filter methods, analytic Gaussian smoothers, particle MCMC, and direct
sufficient-statistic smoothers are alternatives. It is especially convenient
for Monte Carlo EM when the M-step is written to consume complete latent paths.

### 1.2 Point-particle FFBS

An ordinary particle filter approximates the filtering distribution by point
masses,

$$
\widehat p_N(X_t\mid y_{1:t})
=\sum_{i=1}^N w_t^{(i)}\delta_{x_t^{(i)}}(X_t).
$$

Substituting this approximation into the backward kernel gives

$$
\Pr(I_t=i\mid x_{t+1}^*)
\propto
w_t^{(i)}p(x_{t+1}^*\mid x_t^{(i)}).
$$

After drawing $I_t$, the previous state is the stored point
$x_t^{(I_t)}$. This is the standard FFBSi construction developed in the
particle-smoothing literature, notably by
[Godsill, Doucet, and West (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214504000000151),
with theoretical developments including
[Douc et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1214/10-AAP735) and the review by
[Lindsten and Schön (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1561/2200000045).

Tracing the stored forward ancestors also produces approximate joint paths, but
resampling causes path degeneracy: many early states share the same ancestor.
FFBSi instead makes a new backward-weighted choice from all filtering particles
at each time and normally provides much greater early-time diversity. The
backward-selected index is therefore not necessarily the forward resampling
ancestor of the selected particle at the next time.

### 1.3 Linear-Gaussian smoothing and simulation smoothing

For a linear-Gaussian model, the Rauch--Tung--Striebel smoother computes
smoothed Gaussian means and covariances analytically
([Rauch, Tung, and Striebel, 1965](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.3166)). RTS itself
is a moment recursion, not an ancestor sampler. A Gaussian simulation smoother
uses the related conditional Gaussian distribution to draw a complete path:
draw the terminal Gaussian state and then draw each previous state conditional
on the next state.

For

$$
X_t\sim\mathcal N(m_t,P_t),\qquad
X_{t+1}=b_t+A_tX_t+\varepsilon_t,\qquad
\varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,Q_t),
$$

define

$$
a_{t+1}=b_t+A_tm_t,\qquad
R_{t+1}=A_tP_tA_t^{\mathsf T}+Q_t.
$$

Gaussian conditioning gives

$$
X_t\mid X_{t+1}=x_{t+1}^*
\sim\mathcal N(h_t,C_t),
$$

where

$$
J_t=P_tA_t^{\mathsf T}R_{t+1}^{-1},
$$

$$
h_t=m_t+J_t(x_{t+1}^*-a_{t+1}),
$$

and

$$
C_t=P_t-J_tR_{t+1}J_t^{\mathsf T}.
$$

Here $h_t$ is a conditional mean and $C_t$ is a conditional covariance;
neither is a stored particle. A simulation smoother draws a new state from this
Gaussian and uses that draw as the fixed future endpoint in the next backward
iteration.

## 2. Lindsten et al.: fully Rao--Blackwellized particle smoothing

### 2.1 Conditionally linear-Gaussian state partition

Lindsten, Bunch, Särkkä, Schön, and Godsill consider conditionally
linear-Gaussian (CLG) models with a fixed state partition

$$
X_t=(u_t,z_t),
$$

where $u_t$ is the non-Gaussian or particle-represented state and $z_t$ is
linear-Gaussian conditional on $u_{1:t}$. A forward RBPF represents

$$
p(u_{1:t},z_t\mid y_{1:t})
\approx
\sum_{i=1}^N
w_t^{(i)}
\delta_{u_{1:t}^{(i)}}(u_{1:t})
\mathcal N(z_t;\bar z_{t\mid t}^{(i)},P_{t\mid t}^{(i)}).
$$

The particles approximate only $u_{1:t}$. A conditional Kalman filter tracks
the mean and covariance of $z_t$ for each particle trajectory.

### 2.2 Why the RB backward kernel is harder than ordinary FFBS

After $z_t$ is marginalized, the process $u_t$ is generally non-Markovian
conditional on the observations. Consequently, the backward probability for a
candidate $u_{1:t}^{(i)}$ can depend on the complete selected future
trajectory $\widetilde u_{t+1:T}$, not only on
$\widetilde u_{t+1}$. A brute-force calculation would run a conditional
Kalman filter through the future for every candidate at every time and would be
quadratic in $T$.

The paper avoids this cost by propagating backward information-filter
sufficient statistics. At every backward time it evaluates marginalized
weights for the stored RBPF trajectories, selects an index $I_t$, retains
$u_t^{(I_t)}$, and prepends it to the selected nonlinear trajectory. The
linear-Gaussian state remains integrated out while these discrete choices are
made.

### 2.3 Smoothing the Gaussian state

After selecting a nonlinear trajectory $\widetilde u_{1:T}$, the paper makes
an additional conditional forward pass and fuses its Gaussian filtering
statistics with the backward information statistics. This produces

$$
p(z_t\mid \widetilde u_{1:T},y_{1:T})
=\mathcal N(z_t;\widetilde z_{t\mid T},\widetilde P_{t\mid T}).
$$

For parameter estimation one also needs the two-time distribution
$p(z_{t-1:t}\mid\widetilde u_{1:T},y_{1:T})$, or equivalently the lag-one
moments. The M-step can then use analytic sufficient statistics instead of
drawing $z_{1:T}$.

This is why the paper calls its method fully Rao--Blackwellized: the tractable
Gaussian state is marginalized in both the forward and backward directions.
Earlier joint backward simulators, including
[Fong et al. (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1109/78.978393), draw both the particle
and Gaussian states backward. Such draws can still be distributionally valid,
but they introduce avoidable Monte Carlo variance in the tractable subspace.

### 2.4 High-level implications

The principal benefits are lower Monte Carlo variance, better scaling when the
Gaussian subspace is high-dimensional, and direct availability of smoothed
means, covariances, and lag-one moments. The costs are model-specific backward
information recursions, storage of particle histories or equivalent sufficient
statistics, and an M-step that can consume expected sufficient statistics.
The non-Markovian dependence created by marginalization is an important
conceptual point: Rao--Blackwellization reduces sampling dimension, but it can
make the backward discrete-state calculation more involved.

## 3. The current football model

### 3.1 State, dynamics, and observations

The current model has a $2M$-dimensional state containing attack and defence
strengths for $M$ teams. Its OU transition is

$$
X_{t+1}
=\mu_0+\phi_t(X_t-\mu_0)+\varepsilon_t,
\qquad
\phi_t=\exp(-\kappa\,dt_t),
$$

with

$$
\varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,Q_t),\qquad
Q_t=(1-\phi_t^2)(\Gamma_0\otimes B).
$$

The bivariate-Poisson likelihood for day $t$ depends only on the teams that
play on that day. Let $\mathcal O_t$ be those sampled coordinates and
$\mathcal R_t$ the remaining coordinates. Unlike the fixed $u/z$ partition
in Lindsten et al., $\mathcal O_t$ changes with the match schedule.

### 3.2 Meaning of a forward RBPF particle

The filter samples the latent blocks used by the day's likelihood. Conditional
on those sampled values it updates the means of all teams and retains the
remaining uncertainty analytically. At filter-state index $t$, component
$i$ therefore represents

$$
X_t\mid I_t=i,y_{1:t}
\sim
\mathcal N(m_t^{(i)},P_t),
\qquad
P_t=\Gamma_{t\mid t}\otimes B.
$$

The stored `filtered_states.particles.x[t, i]` is $m_t^{(i)}$, not a complete
sample of all $2M$ state coordinates. Coordinates explicitly sampled by the
filter have zero residual rows and columns in $\Gamma_{t\mid t}$; the other
coordinates remain Gaussian. Because the match schedule is shared across
particles, the component covariance is common across $i$.

The forward propagation is correctly Rao--Blackwellized when it propagates the
mean deterministically and carries the process uncertainty through
`gamma_pred`. The absence of a full $2M$-dimensional process-noise draw in
`particles.x` is intentional, not an error.

### 3.3 Filtering-mixture backward kernel

The filtering approximation is

$$
\widehat p_N(X_t\mid y_{1:t})
=\sum_{i=1}^N w_t^{(i)}
\mathcal N(X_t;m_t^{(i)},P_t).
$$

For a complete future draw $x_{t+1}^*$, define

$$
a_{t+1}^{(i)}
=\mu_0+\phi_t(m_t^{(i)}-\mu_0),
$$

$$
R_{t+1}=\phi_t^2P_t+Q_t
=\Gamma_{t+1\mid t}\otimes B.
$$

Integrating over the continuous uncertainty within component $i$ gives the
backward component probabilities

$$
\widetilde w_t^{(i)}
\propto
w_t^{(i)}
\mathcal N(x_{t+1}^*;a_{t+1}^{(i)},R_{t+1}).
$$

After drawing $I_t$, the previous complete state has the conditional Gaussian
distribution

$$
X_t\mid X_{t+1}=x_{t+1}^*,I_t,y_{1:t}
\sim\mathcal N(h_t^{(I_t)},C_t),
$$

where

$$
J_t=P_t\phi_tR_{t+1}^{-1},
$$

$$
h_t^{(i)}
=m_t^{(i)}+J_t(x_{t+1}^*-a_{t+1}^{(i)}),
$$

$$
C_t=P_t-J_tR_{t+1}J_t^{\mathsf T}.
$$

With the Kronecker structure,

$$
J_t=
\left(
\phi_t\Gamma_{t\mid t}\Gamma_{t+1\mid t}^{-1}
\right)\otimes I_2,
$$

and

$$
C_t=
\left[
\Gamma_{t\mid t}
-\phi_t^2\Gamma_{t\mid t}
\Gamma_{t+1\mid t}^{-1}\Gamma_{t\mid t}
\right]\otimes B.
$$

The selected index identifies the particle-represented conditioning history.
The conditional Gaussian draw resolves the uncertainty that was deliberately
not sampled by the forward RBPF. The draw $X_t^*$, not $h_t^{(I_t)}$, is
then used as the fixed future endpoint at the next backward iteration.

### 3.4 Why the generic Cuthbert backward sampler fails

Cuthbert's `exact_sampling.simulate` implements point-particle FFBS. It samples
an index and returns `x0_all[index]`. Its `x1_ancestor_indices` argument is not
used for the selection. Thus the failure is not that Cuthbert traces a forward
ancestor; the failure is that it interprets the selected stored object as a
complete point state.

Passing the RB component means directly causes two errors:

1. The weights use the complete-state innovation covariance $Q_t$, rather
   than the marginalized component prediction covariance
   $R_{t+1}=\phi_t^2P_t+Q_t$.
2. The selected mean $m_t^{(I_t)}$ is returned as the complete state, dropping
   the residual covariance $P_t$.

Independently materializing one full state for every time/component before
smoothing changes the problem but does not solve it. It creates independent
96-dimensional point clouds and asks point FFBS to connect them through the
narrow daily $Q_t$. With only about 100 candidates, compatible pairs are very
unlikely, producing concentrated backward weights, duplicated indices, and
large transition Mahalanobis residuals.

## 4. Comparison of the local documents and literature

### 4.1 `papers/RB_Gueant.md`

`RB_Gueant.md` studies a simplified correlated Gaussian random walk with one
asynchronously observed coordinate at each time. It correctly describes the
forward filter as a Gaussian mixture and, in Section 3.4, selects a mixture
component using the marginalized covariance $P_k+Q$ before drawing the full
previous state from its conditional Gaussian.

This is a valid full-state joint backward simulator for the simplified model.
However, under the terminology of Lindsten et al., it is not fully
Rao--Blackwellized in the backward direction because it samples the tractable
Gaussian state rather than retaining its smoothing distribution analytically.
It is closer to the joint backward-simulation family discussed by Fong et al.

### 4.2 `rbpf/MODEL.md`

`MODEL.md` adapts the `RB_Gueant.md` construction to:

- mean-reverting OU dynamics rather than a random walk;
- a Kronecker covariance $\Gamma\otimes B$;
- multiple asynchronously observed teams grouped by day;
- a nonlinear bivariate-Poisson score likelihood; and
- a Monte Carlo EM M-step that consumes complete state paths.

The random-walk gain in `RB_Gueant.md`,

$$
J_t=P_t(P_t+Q)^{-1},
$$

becomes the OU gain

$$
J_t=P_t\phi_t(\phi_t^2P_t+Q_t)^{-1}.
$$

The smoothing equations in `MODEL.md` are therefore the appropriate OU
generalization. The statement that Cuthbert uses an "exact backward kernel" and
therefore does not need to sample the weighted Gaussian mixture is incorrect
for the RBPF representation. Cuthbert is exact only for the finite point cloud
it is supplied; it does not integrate or condition the Gaussian components.

### 4.3 Three distinct smoothers

| Method | Discrete step | Gaussian step | Suitable output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary point FFBSi | Select a stored point index | None | Point-particle path |
| RB-aware full-state FFBS | Select an RB Gaussian component | Draw $X_t\sim\mathcal N(h_t,C_t)$ | Complete MCEM path |
| Fully RB smoother of Lindsten et al. | Select the particle-represented trajectory | Keep Gaussian state marginalized and compute smoothing moments | Analytic or Rao--Blackwellized EM statistics |

The second method is the correct immediate implementation for the current
path-based M-step. The third method should have lower Monte Carlo variance but
would require a different smoother and an M-step based on
$E[X_t]$, $E[X_tX_t^{\mathsf T}]$, and
$E[X_tX_{t+1}^{\mathsf T}]$. It is a redesign, not a small change to
`backward_sampling_fn`.

## 5. Required RB backward-sampling implementation

### 5.1 Timeline contract

For $D$ observed days, the current filter has $D+1$ states:

```text
filter state                 0, 1, ..., D
transition/day               0, 1, ..., D-1
filtered covariance          gamma_0, gamma[0], ..., gamma[D-1]
prediction for transition d  gamma_pred[d]
```

The transition from state $d$ to state $d+1$ must use

```text
gamma_filtered = [gamma_0, gamma[:-1]][d]
gamma_pred_next = gamma_pred[d]
phi_next = exp(-kappa * dt[d])
```

There must not be an additional `[1:]` shift of `gamma_pred`.

### 5.2 Why metadata must be attached to the particle tree

Cuthbert calls a backward function with

```python
(key, x0_all, x1_all, log_weight_x0_all,
 log_density, x1_ancestor_indices)
```

but does not pass the time-specific filtered and predicted covariances directly.
The callback therefore cannot be correct if `x0_all` contains only `x`. One
Cuthbert-compatible solution is to construct a smoother-only particle tree
whose metadata leaves are broadcast across the component axis. The terminal
resampler must also draw a full terminal state rather than returning a selected
component mean.

The following is the complete reference implementation intended for
`rbpf/src/smoothing.py`. It is documentation only in this review; no source code
has been changed.

```python
from typing import NamedTuple


class RBSmootherParticle(NamedTuple):
    """Smoother-only representation of one forward RB component.

    Every metadata leaf is broadcast across the component axis because
    Cuthbert resamples/indexes every leaf in the particle tree.
    """

    x: jax.Array                    # component mean forward; full draw backward
    gamma_filtered: jax.Array       # Gamma_{t|t}
    gamma_pred_next: jax.Array      # Gamma_{t+1|t}
    phi_next: jax.Array             # OU coefficient for t -> t+1


def _sample_kron_psd(key, mean, gamma, B):
    """Draw an array with covariance gamma kron B."""

    noise = jax.random.normal(key, mean.shape)
    L_gamma = _psd_sqrt(gamma)
    L_B = _psd_sqrt(B)
    return mean + L_gamma @ noise @ L_B.T


def make_rb_smoother_filter_states(filtered_states, rb_inputs, params):
    """Attach the covariance/timing metadata required by the callback.

    filtered_states.particles.x has shape (D+1, N, teams, 2).
    rb_inputs.gamma and rb_inputs.gamma_pred have shape (D, teams, teams).
    """

    means = jax.lax.stop_gradient(filtered_states.particles.x)
    n_states, n_components = means.shape[:2]

    filtered_gamma = jnp.concatenate(
        [params.gamma_0[None], rb_inputs.gamma], axis=0
    )
    dt = rb_inputs.timestamp - rb_inputs.timestamp_prev
    phi = jnp.exp(-params.kappa * dt)

    if n_states != dt.size + 1:
        raise ValueError("Expected D+1 filter states for D transitions")
    if filtered_gamma.shape[0] != n_states:
        raise ValueError("Filtered covariance timeline is misaligned")
    if rb_inputs.gamma_pred.shape[0] != dt.size:
        raise ValueError("Predicted covariance timeline is misaligned")

    # The terminal entries are dummies: no transition leaves terminal state D.
    gamma_pred_with_terminal = jnp.concatenate(
        [rb_inputs.gamma_pred, rb_inputs.gamma_pred[-1:]], axis=0
    )
    phi_with_terminal = jnp.concatenate([phi, phi[-1:]], axis=0)

    def broadcast_components(values):
        return jnp.broadcast_to(
            values[:, None],
            (n_states, n_components) + values.shape[1:],
        )

    smoother_particles = RBSmootherParticle(
        x=means,
        gamma_filtered=broadcast_components(filtered_gamma),
        gamma_pred_next=broadcast_components(gamma_pred_with_terminal),
        phi_next=broadcast_components(phi_with_terminal),
    )
    return filtered_states._replace(particles=smoother_particles)


def rb_terminal_resampling(key, logits, positions, n, *, B):
    """Select terminal components and materialize their full terminal states."""

    key, index_key, state_key = jax.random.split(key, 3)
    indices, output_logits, selected = (
        cuthbertlib.resampling.systematic.resampling(
            index_key, logits, positions, n
        )
    )
    state_keys = jax.random.split(state_key, n)
    terminal_states = jax.vmap(
        lambda draw_key, mean, gamma: _sample_kron_psd(
            draw_key, mean, gamma, B
        )
    )(
        state_keys,
        selected.x,
        selected.gamma_filtered,
    )
    return indices, output_logits, selected._replace(x=terminal_states)


def rb_backward_sampling_fn(
    key,
    x0_all,
    x1_all,
    log_weight_x0_all,
    log_density,
    x1_ancestor_indices,
    *,
    mean_0,
    B,
):
    """Sample previous full states from the RB backward kernel.

    x0_all.x contains all N forward RB component means at state t.
    x1_all.x contains the already-sampled full states at state t+1.

    `log_density` is intentionally unused: it describes a point-state
    transition and cannot integrate over the RB component covariance.
    `x1_ancestor_indices` is also intentionally unused: this is backward
    component selection, not forward genealogy tracing.
    """

    del log_density, x1_ancestor_indices

    component_means = x0_all.x
    component_log_weights = jnp.asarray(log_weight_x0_all)
    n_components = component_means.shape[0]
    dimension = component_means[0].size

    # These values are shared by all components at time t. They were
    # broadcast only to satisfy the particle-tree indexing contract.
    gamma_t = x0_all.gamma_filtered[0]
    gamma_pred_next = x0_all.gamma_pred_next[0]
    phi_t = x0_all.phi_next[0]

    predicted_means = (
        mean_0[None]
        + phi_t * (component_means - mean_0[None])
    )

    log_determinant = _kron_logdet(gamma_pred_next, B)
    log_normalization = (
        -0.5 * dimension * jnp.log(2.0 * jnp.pi)
        -0.5 * log_determinant
    )

    # J_gamma kron I_2 is the full RTS gain. Compute the solve once because
    # gamma_t and gamma_pred_next are common to all mixture components.
    J_gamma = phi_t * jnp.linalg.solve(
        gamma_pred_next, gamma_t.T
    ).T
    conditional_gamma = (
        gamma_t
        - J_gamma @ gamma_pred_next @ J_gamma.T
    )
    conditional_gamma = 0.5 * (
        conditional_gamma + conditional_gamma.T
    )

    def sample_one(trajectory_key, x_next):
        component_key, state_key = jax.random.split(trajectory_key)

        residuals = (
            x_next[None] - predicted_means
        ).reshape(n_components, -1)
        quadratic = _kron_quad(
            gamma_pred_next,
            B,
            residuals,
        )
        backward_logits = (
            component_log_weights
            + log_normalization
            - 0.5 * quadratic
        )

        component_index = jax.random.categorical(
            component_key, backward_logits
        )
        future_error = (
            x_next - predicted_means[component_index]
        )
        conditional_mean = (
            component_means[component_index]
            + J_gamma @ future_error
        )
        x_previous = _sample_kron_psd(
            state_key,
            conditional_mean,
            conditional_gamma,
            B,
        )
        return x_previous, component_index

    n_trajectories = x1_all.x.shape[0]
    trajectory_keys = jax.random.split(key, n_trajectories)
    previous_states, component_indices = jax.vmap(sample_one)(
        trajectory_keys, x1_all.x
    )

    # Preserve the selected component metadata while replacing its stored mean
    # by the newly sampled full conditional state.
    selected = jax.tree.map(
        lambda values: values[component_indices],
        x0_all,
    )
    selected = selected._replace(x=previous_states)
    return selected, component_indices
```

### 5.3 Numerical and validation requirements

Before JIT compilation, the implementation should verify:

1. Every recorded `dt` is strictly positive.
2. Every `gamma_pred[d]` is positive definite; use a Cholesky solve when
   practical rather than silently applying a pseudoinverse.
3. `gamma_filtered` and `conditional_gamma` may be positive semidefinite
   because sampled coordinates have exactly zero residual variance. Their
   square-root routine should clip only roundoff-scale negative eigenvalues and
   report materially negative eigenvalues.
4. The component weights, $h_t^{(i)}$, and $C_t$ agree with a direct dense
   Gaussian-conditioning calculation in a small model.
5. Smoothed means, variances, and lag-one covariances agree, within Monte Carlo
   error, with a Kalman/RTS reference in a small linear-Gaussian example.
6. The transition Mahalanobis ratio is order one and stable with increasing
   particle count, rather than remaining near 27.
7. Backward component diversity does not persistently collapse to a single
   index.

The existing complete-state `joint_log_potential` and
`log_transition_density` should continue to use $Q_t$. They evaluate
$p(X_{t+1}\mid X_t)$ once complete states are available. Only the discrete
RB component-selection weights use the marginalized prediction covariance
$R_{t+1}=\phi_t^2P_t+Q_t$, represented in code by `gamma_pred_next`.
